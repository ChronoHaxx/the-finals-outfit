## The drift question and the rule (option A)

The drift gate (`s12-definition-drift.py`, map v1) on the 53 items that show the six batch C meshes found:
- 18 identical;
- 35 differing only in `VisualParts`.

In each of the 35, the 17 Nov build appended one TagOverride to the part:
- `MatchingTags ["Customization.Feature.MeshMergeModifiers"]`;
- swap to a suffix-less mesh (`SK_Streetwear_Hoodie` for `SK_Streetwear_Hoodie_M`);
- no materials.

Our resolver needs every matching tag and never has that one, so the override is inactive.

The coordinator approved option A on 26 Nov "as a narrow neutral-drift rule in code". It is in PR #109,
`scripts/shader-probe/merge-modifier-drift.mjs`, with tests. An item is admitted only when both hold:
1. **Structural:** the 17 Nov VisualParts equal the stored ones plus appended overrides that match exactly that one tag
   and only swap the mesh:
   - no material, effect, logic-module, offset or wrap-deformer override;
   - no existing override changed;
   - no other part field changed.
2. **Resolver:** `resolveSourceOutfit` gives the same result on Medium for the stored and the 17 Nov definitions. The
   resolver's raw `definition` echo is left out of the comparison, because that echo is the drift itself.

Whether the game grants the tag, and what the suffix-less meshes are, was a separate investigation (PR #110,
`_docs/meshmerge-modifiers-2026-11-26/`). It found that no readable data grants the tag (the grant is code-side), and
that the suffix-less meshes serve all classes and body types through a modifier stack. The rule makes no claim about
the game; it says only that our resolver draws the same outfit from either definition.

`c1-run/prepare-cohort.mjs` applies the rule per item and records each verdict in the cohort (`driftAdmission`, and
`mergeModifierAdmitted` with the rule's sha256). The batch still builds from the stored definitions; the runner
re-checks them against the freeze.

### Inverse binds vs our skeleton (diagnostic, 26 Nov 2026)

The question: do the reweighted joints' inverse binds disagree with the rest pose our rig binds to? If so, the coat
creases and the cropped-tee wedge (below) would be a viewer or converter root, not the game's weights.

Script and report: `scripts/generated/s12-refresh-tier2-v1/c1-vpj-binds/` (read-only).
- For each changed joint, it compares `restWorld @ IBM` against identity. `restWorld` is the active index's
  `attachmentBody.restBones`.
- It checks the live and S12 files of the tee, LongCoat and CropTopHoodie.

| Mesh | Vertices reweighted | Joints changed |
|---|---:|---:|
| CroppedTshirtOversize | 1,831 | 25 |
| LongCoat | 275 | 20 |
| CropTopHoodie | 3,581 | 41 |

The binds agree:
- Every changed spine, `chest_vpj`, clavicle, upper-arm, `upperarm_vpj_01..03` and lower-arm joint is within 0.000 cm
  and 0.04°, in both the S12 and the live files.
- The only real offset is `thigh_roll_vpj_l/r`, about 1.0 cm and 4°; `thigh_front/back_vpj` are under 1 mm and
  about 0.1°. These offsets are identical in live and S12, so they predate Season 12, and the changed weight there is
  under 0.1.

So the creases and the wedge come from the game's own weights as our rig poses them, and the holds stand.
